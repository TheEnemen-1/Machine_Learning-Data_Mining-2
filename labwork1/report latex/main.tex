\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}
This labwork studies data preparation, statistical relationships and principal component analysis (PCA) on the public Stroke Prediction Dataset and Spotify Dataset. Both have more than six input features, as required by the assignment~\cite{assignment}. The analysis first establishes the representation and quality of each dataset, then examines the variance retained by lower-dimensional projections. Labels are used to describe groups in plots, not to fit PCA.

\section{Stroke Prediction Dataset}
\subsection{Dataset Overview}
The Stroke Prediction Dataset is distributed by fedesoriano on Kaggle~\cite{stroke-data}. The local file \texttt{healthcare-dataset-stroke-data.csv} contains \textbf{5,110 records} and 12 columns. Excluding the internal identifier \texttt{id} and target \texttt{stroke} leaves \textbf{ten input variables}. Each row is an observation and each column an attribute, following the matrix representation introduced in the lectures~\cite{dm2,dm3}.

The attributes describe demographic characteristics, recorded health conditions and lifestyle information. The file is analyzed as a static cross-sectional benchmark table. It supplies no timestamps or follow-up windows, and its schema does not establish population origin, sampling protocols or the original study design. Results therefore describe this dataset rather than prospective clinical risk or causal effects.

\subsubsection{Data integration}
Additional clinical or environmental records could provide context, but no compatible linkage is supplied. The raw \texttt{id} has no external registry mapping; Rural/Urban residence categories are not geographic keys, and timestamps and hospital identifiers are absent. Integration is investigated but not performed, consistent with the assignment's conditional requirement. No specific privacy-law process is inferred from the schema, and unsupported joins could introduce misleading associations.

\subsubsection{Transformation and scaling}
Binary mapping represents gender, ever-married status and residence with one column each. Full one-hot encoding represents the five work types and four smoking categories without imposing an order. Together with the other inputs, this yields \textbf{17 encoded columns}. Mean-centering is part of covariance-based PCA; scaling is a separate choice~\cite{dm2,dm3}. Here, glucose sample variance is 2050.73, more than 8,000 times the approximate upper bound 0.25 for binary-feature variance. We therefore use
\begin{align}
z_{ij}&=\frac{x_{ij}-\mu_j}{\sigma_j}, &
\mu_j&=\frac1n\sum_i x_{ij}, &
\sigma_j^2&=\frac1n\sum_i(x_{ij}-\mu_j)^2.
\end{align}
The implementation uses \texttt{StandardScaler}~\cite{sklearn}, with denominator $n$. All 17 columns have means approximately zero and variance one under this convention. Min--max scaling, $(x-x_{\min})/(x_{\max}-x_{\min})$, is an alternative, but its endpoints depend on extremes. Neither transformation makes PCA insensitive to outliers.

Encoding changes weighting as well as representation. Work type and smoking status contribute nine of 17 standardized columns, or 52.94\% of total variance before PCA. Equal variance per column is not equal weight per original variable. For example, the Never\_worked dummy occurs 22 times, reaches 15.21 when active after standardization, and has a PC7 coefficient of approximately 0.78.

\subsubsection{Statistical measures and categorical associations}
The numerical summaries in Table~\ref{tab:stroke-stats} use the empirical first raw moment and sample variance~\cite{dm25,dm3}:
\[
\bar x=\frac1n\sum_i x_i,\qquad
s^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2,\qquad s=\sqrt{s^2}.
\]
Means alone do not describe skewness or the mixture of pediatric and adult records, and a mean BMI or glucose value is not a diagnosis. Nominal variables are summarized through modes, frequencies and contingency tables rather than numerical means.

For observed frequencies $O_{rc}$ and expected counts $E_{rc}=N_{r\cdot}N_{\cdot c}/n$, Pearson's independence statistic and its degrees of freedom are
\[
\chi^2=\sum_{r,c}\frac{(O_{rc}-E_{rc})^2}{E_{rc}},\qquad df=(R-1)(C-1).
\]
The implemented $2\times2$ tests use Yates' continuity correction:
\[
\chi_Y^2=\sum_{r,c}\frac{\max(|O_{rc}-E_{rc}|-0.5,0)^2}{E_{rc}}.
\]
Tables~\ref{tab:stroke-categories} and~\ref{tab:stroke-rates} report the tests and empirical rates. Stroke prevalence is 17.03\% with heart disease versus 4.18\% without, and 13.25\% with hypertension versus 3.97\% without. These are associations, not causal or confounding-adjusted effects. Work type has a minimum expected count of 1.07, limiting the asymptotic interpretation. For gender ($p=0.560$) and residence ($p=0.300$), there is insufficient evidence to reject independence at 0.05; identical prevalence has not been proved. The tests are exploratory and unadjusted for age, other covariates or multiple comparisons.

\subsubsection{Covariance and correlation}
Sample covariance and Pearson correlation are
\[
s_{xy}=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y),\qquad r_{xy}=\frac{s_{xy}}{s_xs_y}.
\]
With $Z=X_{\mathrm{scaled}}$, population variance one per column gives
\begin{equation}
R=\frac1nZ^TZ,\qquad C=\frac1{n-1}Z^TZ
=\frac{n}{n-1}R\approx1.00019577R.
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:stroke-covariance} shows that the standardized sample covariance diagonal is approximately 1.0002. The common factor leaves eigenvectors and EVRs unchanged.

\subsection{Principal Component Analysis}
\subsubsection{Computation and numerical checks}
Following the lecture procedure~\cite{dm3}, PCA forms the covariance of centered data, computes its eigenvectors and selects the directions of greatest variance:
\begin{align}
C&=\frac1{n-1}Z^TZ,& C\mathbf v_j&=\lambda_j\mathbf v_j,\\
V^TV&=I,& \lambda_1&\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d\ge0,\qquad Y_p=ZV_p.
\end{align}
The fitted solver policy is \texttt{auto}, which selects \texttt{covariance\_eigh} in the recorded environment. Manual covariance eigendecomposition agrees with the fitted eigenvalues within $1.1794\times10^{-15}$; the orthonormality discrepancy is $2.6645\times10^{-15}$. These are floating-point consistency checks, not an independent full-SVD benchmark. SVD provides another route,
\[
Z=USV^T,\qquad \lambda_j=\frac{s_j^2}{n-1},
\]
and can avoid explicitly forming a $d\times d$ covariance matrix when $d$ is large.

\subsubsection{Explained variance and component selection}
For this covariance convention,
\begin{align}
\operatorname{tr}(C)&=\sum_{j=1}^d\lambda_j=d\frac{n}{n-1}\approx17.0033,\\
\mathrm{EVR}_j&=\frac{\lambda_j}{\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda_k},&
\mathrm{CEVR}_p&=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^p\lambda_j}{\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda_k}.
\end{align}
Table~\ref{tab:stroke-evr} reports percentages from these ratios. Figure~\ref{fig:stroke-scree} declines gradually without a sharp single elbow. The primary choice is the smallest dimension reaching 80\% variance: \textbf{ten PCs} retain \textbf{82.21\%} and reduce columns by 41.2\%. \textbf{Twelve PCs} reach the 90\% target with \textbf{92.08\%} retention and 29.4\% reduction, consistent with the lecture's 80--90\% guidance~\cite{dm3}.

Kaiser's rule is a comparison, retaining seven PCs and 65.69\% variance. Its reference of one concerns standardized-variable variance in correlation PCA, not an eigenvalue assigned to each original feature. Here the equivalent threshold is $\lambda_j>n/(n-1)\approx1.0002$. Full one-hot groups each sum to one; after centering/scaling, each gives a constraint $\sum_k\sigma_kz_k=0$. The covariance rank is 15, producing two theoretically zero-variance directions, PC16 and PC17. These are degeneracies rather than evidence of noise. High variance does not guarantee target relevance, and low non-zero variance does not imply zero association. PCA is feature extraction, not selection of original variables~\cite{dm25}.

\subsubsection{Varying the retained dimension and reconstruction}
All projections and reconstructions use the same fitted basis:
\begin{align}
Y_p&=ZV_p,& \hat Z&=Y_pV_p^T,\\
\mathrm{MSE}(p)&=\frac1{nd}\lVert Z-\hat Z\rVert_F^2
=\frac{n-1}{nd}\sum_{j=p+1}^d\lambda_j
=1-\mathrm{CEVR}_p.
\end{align}
CEVR is a ratio in $[0,1]$. The final equality uses unit population variance in every standardized column; it is not a general identity for unscaled data. The implementation adds the fitted mean to account for residual floating-point centering.

Table~\ref{tab:stroke-reconstruction} and Figure~\ref{fig:stroke-reconstruction} show the trade-off. At ten PCs the MSE is \textbf{0.17793}; empirical and corrected theoretical errors differ by at most $2.2204\times10^{-16}$. Retained variance and MSE are mathematically coupled, not independent evidence of predictive quality. Fifteen PCs recover the preprocessed standardized matrix to numerical precision, without restoring missing BMI, uncapped values or the excluded record.

\[
\operatorname{Corr}(Z_k,Y_j)=\frac{v_{kj}\sqrt{\lambda_j}}{\sqrt{n/(n-1)}}.
\]
It is undefined at $\lambda_j=0$. The weights, scaled loadings and correlations are related but not interchangeable.

\subsubsection{Transformation and scaling}
The transformation demonstration applies $\log(1+\mathrm{duration\_ms})$, then standardizes log duration, loudness and tempo. The resulting sample SDs are 1.000006, consistent with population-based scaling followed by a sample-statistic display. Seven bounded descriptors are min--max scaled; explicit is mapped to 0/1; key is encoded by
\[
k_{\sin}=\sin(2\pi k/12),\qquad k_{\cos}=\cos(2\pi k/12).
\]
This encodes pitch-class adjacency, not every aspect of harmonic similarity. Genre is label-encoded as a target, while time signature is one-hot encoded with one reference level. Log transformation compresses the duration tail but does not establish normality.

These demonstration columns must be distinguished from the actual PCA input. The PCA code selects the \emph{original cleaned} duration, danceability, energy, loudness, speechiness, acousticness, instrumentalness, liveness, valence and tempo, and z-score standardizes all ten. It does not use the log-duration column, genre codes, time-signature dummies or simulated artist attributes. Scaling is appropriate for these mixed measurement scales, rather than a universal requirement for every PCA.

\subsubsection{Summary of data preparation}
Cleaning yields a distinct-track analytical table under a first-occurrence rule and reduces extreme duration/loudness values. The resulting representation is conditional on filtering choices and may underrepresent atypical recordings. Integration is a synthetic join demonstration with row multiplication, not evidence of richer measured data. Transformation examples illustrate alternative representations, while the actual PCA consistently uses ten standardized original audio columns. These distinctions prevent attributing results to preprocessing steps that were not used in the PCA fit.

\subsection{Principal Component Analysis}
\subsubsection{Preprocessing and computation}
PCA is fitted on all 85,905 cleaned records using ten standardized numerical audio inputs. Metadata, genre, popularity and synthetic artist variables are excluded. The computation follows the same centering, covariance and eigenvector formulation used for Stroke. With population scaling and sample covariance, the diagonal is $n/(n-1)$ and EVRs use the sum of eigenvalues. The model is fitted without genre labels; labels are used only after fitting for visualization.

\subsubsection{Explained variance and component selection}

Reconstruction is assessed through retained variance; no separate inverse-transform MSE experiment is performed for Spotify. For these standardized inputs, the spectral identity established above gives the theoretical relationship
\[
\mathrm{MSE}(p)=\frac{n-1}{nd}\sum_{j>p}\lambda_j=1-\mathrm{CEVR}_p.
\]
This explains the reconstruction trade-off; it is not an additional empirical validation. Retaining all ten directions permits reconstruction to numerical precision, whereas retaining fewer discards measured variance.

\subsubsection{Critical evaluation}
Original audio measurements have interpretable units or descriptor scales. PCA scores instead mix these inputs into uncorrelated coordinates, losing direct meanings such as a tempo in beats per minute. The ten-to-six reduction preserves most variance under the chosen criterion, but discarded variance may include genre-related variation. Table~\ref{tab:spotify-evaluation} separates these observed results from untested claims about noise removal or downstream performance. Nonlinear visualization and predictive comparisons would require further experiments.
